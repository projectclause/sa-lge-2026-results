The ANC loses its grip on the metros, but keeps two mayors through coalition deals

The ANC won no metro outright. Through coalitions it kept the mayor’s office in Ekurhuleni and Buffalo City, and took the speaker’s chair in Johannesburg, Tshwane and eThekwini while backing ActionSA and MK mayors.

Preamble’s AI summary of 33 reports. Updated 28 November 2026.

In brief

  • The new mayors: ActionSA in Johannesburg and Tshwane, the ANC in Ekurhuleni and Buffalo City, the DA in Cape Town and Nelson Mandela Bay, and MK in eThekwini
  • The ANC’s speakers include Adolph Marema in Johannesburg, Naeem Patel in Tshwane and Thabani Nyawose in eThekwini
  • The ANC’s Thapelo Radebe is Johannesburg’s deputy mayor
  • Before the vote, Du Toit expected it to confirm the ANC’s faster decline, and Basson noted the party would not control a single metro
  • The Mail & Guardian writes that the ANC limps on with diminished appeal

Before the vote

On 31 October, Pieter du Toit argued that the municipal election could confirm that the ANC’s decline was speeding up.

After the count

On 5 November he wrote that the ANC was on the brink of falling to second, third or fourth place in seven of the eight metros. The same day, Adriaan Basson noted that the party would not control a single metro.

After the mayoral votes

By 27 November most metros had elected their mayors. The ANC kept Ekurhuleni and Buffalo City, and in Johannesburg, Tshwane and eThekwini it chose to back ActionSA and MK mayors in exchange for the speaker’s chair and other posts, rather than share power with the DA.
