ActionSA bargains with both big parties, then leans towards the ANC

ActionSA’s chair Michael Beaumont spent November talking to both the DA and the ANC, arguing that coalitions, not vote share, decide who governs.

Preamble’s AI summary of 82 reports. Updated 27 November 2026.

In brief

  • Talks with the ANC began on 8 November
  • Beaumont said progress was being made with both the DA and ANC
  • He called the ANC’s offer attractive
  • He said ActionSA serves at the pleasure of no other party
  • ActionSA says its problem is with Zille and Brink, not the DA, and will back the DA’s mayoral candidate in Nelson Mandela Bay, EWN reports
  • Mashaba says the ball is now in the ANC’s court and ActionSA will respect whatever its NEC decides

Talking to both sides

Beaumont described ActionSA’s first meeting with the ANC’s Fikile Mbalula and Nomvula Mokonyane on 8 November as productive and respectful. On 17 November he said talks with both the DA and the ANC were making progress, and argued that where no party has a majority, coalitions decide who governs.

The tilt towards the ANC

On 702 on 18 November he called the ANC’s offer attractive and said the DA had never really held talks with ActionSA. By 22 November he was pushing back at DA supporters, saying ActionSA serves at the pleasure of no other party and is set to lead Tshwane again and take over in Johannesburg.
