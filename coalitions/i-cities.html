South Africa’s cities move to coalition rule

Straight after the results, Bloomberg and Reuters reported that the biggest cities would be run by coalitions, and AP described high-stakes talks after the ANC’s worst result. Two weeks on, Bloomberg’s S’thembile Cele wrote that post-election bartering was counting for more than votes, while the FT and the BBC reported the ANC fighting to keep control of key cities.

Preamble’s AI summary of 24 reports. Updated 23 November 2026.

In brief

  • Bloomberg reported on 8 November that South Africa’s cities faced coalition rule as voters spurned the ANC
  • Reuters reported the ANC took a beating, setting up tough coalition talks
  • AP reported high-stakes coalition talks after the ANC’s worst results
  • The FT and the BBC reported the ANC fighting to keep control of key cities
  • Bloomberg later wrote that post-election bartering was counting for more than votes

The angle abroad

Foreign outlets explain coalition rule as something new for readers who know South Africa as a one-party story. Their pieces describe hung councils as a sign of a fragmenting vote rather than as a contest between named candidates.

The bargaining

By the third week, the framing had shifted from the ANC’s losses to the deal-making. Bloomberg’s S’thembile Cele wrote that bartering after the vote was mattering more than the vote itself.
