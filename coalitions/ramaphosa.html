South Africa · National · South Africa

In public, Ramaphosa asks for good faith. In private, insiders say he wants the DA kept out of Joburg

The president told all parties to bargain in good faith and form councils quickly. A report based on unnamed insiders says he steered the ANC away from the DA in Johannesburg, which the ANC denies.

What he said in public

At the IEC results announcement on 8 November, Ramaphosa said hung councils should not be read as a failure of democracy, and urged parties to negotiate in good faith rather than fight over positions and power. In his weekly newsletter the next day, he said councillors must find common ground from the start so councils can begin working.

What insiders say he said

On 11 November the Sunday Times reported, citing unnamed insiders, that Ramaphosa told a closed ANC meeting to explore a Johannesburg coalition without the DA. ANC spokesperson Mahlengi Bhengu dismissed claims that he had stated a preference. Preamble has not seen this confirmed on the record.

The NEC decides

Before the meeting, Business Day’s Hajra Omarjee reported Luthuli House sources saying Ramaphosa had told the party to avoid coalitions with the DA and treat one as a last resort, with opponents of the four-party deal expecting to be in the minority. Late on Monday, News24 reported that the national executive committee had handed the power to choose coalition partners to its provinces and regions, which have very different preferences on whom to align with.
