South Africa · MK Party · eThekwini, KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng

MK talked to everyone, the DA included, then took eThekwini with the ANC

MK came third nationally and leads many KwaZulu-Natal councils. Its negotiator Dali Mpofu met the DA for the first time, but MK settled on the ANC: its Mxolisi Phakathi is now eThekwini’s mayor.

Preamble’s AI summary of 65 reports. Updated 27 November 2026.

In brief

  • MK’s Mxolisi Phakathi was elected eThekwini mayor with the ANC’s support, and MK’s Thabani Luthuli deputy mayor
  • MK says its near-10% vote shows voters want parties to cooperate
  • Deals must rest on shared policy and be done within 100 days
  • Mpofu called MK’s first meeting with the DA historic
  • But MK will focus on like-minded parties
  • Talks with the ANC, EFF and ActionSA continue
  • KZN Cogta told eThekwini and 12 other hung councils to move Tuesday’s first meetings to 26 or 27 November, but eThekwini is going ahead on Tuesday. A source told IOL MK would take the mayoralty, ActionSA the deputy mayor post and the ANC the speaker

The ground rules

On 7 November, Dali Mpofu said MK’s near-10% national vote shows voters want parties to cooperate. Any deal, he said, must rest on shared policy and be concluded within 100 days of a council being set up, with MK’s leadership having the final say.

A warning to the ANC

On 12 November, Mpofu warned that the ANC working with parties whose interests oppose its own could lead to its demise, while saying MK did not want the talks to become a slanging match.

Talking to the DA

Around 14 November, Mpofu described MK’s first-ever meeting with the DA as historic, and confirmed that DA leader Geordin Hill-Lewis had asked for the talks. But he said MK will focus on like-minded parties. By 19 November, MK’s talks with the ANC, EFF and ActionSA were continuing, including in eThekwini.

A split in the ANC

News24 reports, citing sources, that Fikile Mbalula is pushing the MK deal on KwaZulu-Natal while the province’s ANC leaders lean towards an ANC-DA-IFP coalition, ahead of a national executive meeting on Monday. EWN reports the ANC and MK are expected to discuss sharing power, including in eThekwini, where MK took 38% of the vote.

eThekwini sits

On Tuesday morning, Thabiso Goba reported for EWN that eThekwini would hold its inaugural meeting that day despite the provincial directive, with all 224 councillors to be sworn in and a speaker, mayor, deputy mayor and chief whip on the agenda. eNCA reported that MK, the largest party in the hung council, is in a potentially influential position.
