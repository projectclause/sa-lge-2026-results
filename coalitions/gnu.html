Will the coalition fights break the national unity government?

Adriaan Basson first expected the DA and ANC to copy their national partnership in the councils. By mid-November he was warning that the fight over the cities could bring it down.

Preamble’s AI summary of 26 reports. Updated 25 November 2026.

In brief

  • Basson set out four possible coalition line-ups
  • He expected a DA-ANC deal to run most of the 138 hung councils
  • He later warned the GNU could collapse if the ANC shut the DA out
  • His latest column asks whether a Doomsday pact is Ramaphosa’s parting shot

Four ways it could go

On 7 November, Basson set out four coalition line-ups that could decide who runs South Africa’s municipalities. On X he named them Ukraine, Lesotho, Rainbow and Ghana, three of them after national flags with the same colours.

GNU 2.0

Two days later he argued that a deal led by the DA and ANC, modelled on the national unity government, was the most likely way the 138 hung councils would be run.

The risk to the national government

By 16 November he was warning that the unity government could collapse if the ANC cut the DA out of city governments. On 21 November, as ANC and MK leaders met in Johannesburg, he asked whether a Doomsday pact was Ramaphosa’s parting shot.

A collision course

On 19 November, Pieter du Toit wrote that the DA and ANC are now on a collision course ahead of the 2029 national election.
