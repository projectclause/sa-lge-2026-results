Abroad, Johannesburg is the story: old foes unite to make Mashaba mayor

Johannesburg gets the most attention outside South Africa. When the council elected Herman Mashaba on 27 November, Bloomberg wrote that old foes had united to return the cosmetics tycoon as mayor, the FT called him a haircare tycoon, and The New York Times stressed that councillors snubbed the party with the most votes.

Preamble’s AI summary of 11 reports. Updated 27 November 2026.

In brief

  • Bloomberg reports that old foes united to return the cosmetics tycoon Herman Mashaba as Johannesburg’s mayor
  • Reuters reports Johannesburg picked Mashaba in an ANC-backed deal, and the FT calls him a haircare tycoon
  • The New York Times frames the vote as councillors snubbing the top vote-getter, the DA
  • Before the vote, Reuters and the FT reported the ANC and its partners nearing a deal to sideline the DA, and the FT that Helen Zille had told allies her bid was over
  • For foreign readers, the story is that the party with the most votes does not govern Africa’s richest city

The angle abroad

Foreign outlets tell the Johannesburg story through the paradox at its centre: the DA won the most votes but may be shut out of power. Reuters puts that paradox in its headline.

What they leave out

Judging by their headlines, foreign outlets give less room than South African ones to the share-out of mayoral posts or the timing of council sittings. They focus on who ends up in charge and what that says about the ANC.
