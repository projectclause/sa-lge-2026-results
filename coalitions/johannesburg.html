South Africa · Johannesburg · City of Johannesburg

Joburg will elect its mayor on Friday as parties finish their deal

All 270 councillors were sworn in on Monday, but the election of a mayor, deputy mayor and speaker now waits until Friday, 27 November. The ANC, ActionSA, EFF and MK deal that would make Mashaba mayor is in its final stages but still to be signed.

Preamble’s AI summary of 159 reports. Updated 25 November 2026.

In brief

  • Helen Zille, Herman Mashaba and the rest of the 270 councillors were sworn in on Monday
  • Parties agreed to postpone the election of the speaker, mayor and deputy mayor; EWN now expects the mayor to be elected on Friday, 27 November
  • Ekurhuleni’s council sits on Wednesday and Tshwane’s on Thursday, EWN reports
  • MK said it would be back to elect Mashaba; other parties said they would not vote for Zille
  • The DA had asked for a postponement; Zille told eNCA it would not delay the council through the courts
  • The ANC, ActionSA, EFF and MK together hold 137 of 270 seats, one more than a majority, The National reports
  • The deal would make Mashaba mayor; IOL and Sunday World report MK’s Bongani Baloyi is expected to be his deputy

An early opening for Zille

On 4 November, Pieter du Toit wrote that a Zille-led city working with the ANC’s moderates was not far-fetched, though it would be very hard to negotiate.

Zille becomes the sticking point

On election night, Herman Mashaba told Clement Manyathela on 702 that he would not negotiate with a DA team that included Zille. On 17 November, Michael Beaumont named the two parties’ Gauteng mayoral candidates as the sticking point. The next day DA leader Geordin Hill-Lewis told Manyathela the party would not drop Zille and would accept opposition if it came to that, while Beaumont called the ANC’s offer attractive.

Swap talk

On 18 November, Hill-Lewis dismissed as dramatic the reports that he had offered to step down as Cape Town mayor, a claim Manyathela put to him on 702 the same day. Two days later Beaumont posted a video arguing that Zille had broken a working coalition in Johannesburg before and would not get a second chance.

Zille concedes

On 18 November, Zille told DA caucus members the die seemed to be cast and the party should prepare for opposition. She said she believed an ANC-ActionSA-EFF-MK coalition would be unstable, but that the DA could still help Johannesburg have a working government. The DA said this was not its official position.

ActionSA moves on

Mashaba told the Mail & Guardian that talks with the DA stalled because it would not drop Zille and Cilliers Brink, and that ActionSA was drafting an agreement with the other parties. Adriaan Basson wrote that the party with the most votes in the city was heading for opposition, and Beaumont said ActionSA was set to take over in Johannesburg.

Decision day

On 21 November, Beaumont told SABC News that ActionSA was 90 to 95% of the way to a deal with the ANC, EFF and MK that would make Mashaba mayor of Johannesburg and keep Nasiphi Moya in Tshwane, though nothing was signed and ActionSA’s senate would decide on Sunday evening. The same day Hill-Lewis made the DA’s final offer and called ActionSA’s demand that Zille be dropped extortion, while Mashaba told IOL the DA had tried to buy his support, which the DA declined to answer.

Monday, 23 November

On Sunday, Inside Politics reported that Mashaba had made himself available for mayor ahead of a council sitting on Monday, while a source said no formal agreement had yet been reached. On Monday morning, Ferial Haffajee reported in Daily Maverick that the ANC, ActionSA, MK and EFF would form the core of the governments in Johannesburg, Tshwane and Ekurhuleni, and that Mashaba was likely to be inaugurated this week. She reported that the ANC wants to keep the finance portfolio and that the arrangement still had to be confirmed by a special meeting of the ANC’s national executive committee on Monday. The National reported that the council was due to sit at 1pm at the Constance Connie Bapela Council Chamber in Braamfontein, and that it was uncertain whether a mayor would be elected at the sitting. Beaumont told EWN the council would most likely be adjourned to Wednesday or so for the mayoral election, and EWN reported that the coalition agreement is likely to be signed on Tuesday. eNCA reported that the ANC’s national executive committee would meet on Monday to consider a report from its negotiating team and give it further direction. Later in the morning, The National reported that the DA had asked the city to postpone the sitting and had threatened legal action after city manager Floyd Brink rejected an earlier request, arguing that the first meeting need not be held before 27 November. It put the combined strength of the ANC, ActionSA, EFF and MK at 137 seats, against the 136 needed, and said MK was expected to take the deputy mayor post, with the agreement expected to be signed on Tuesday.

Tuesday, 24 November

Early on Tuesday, Alpha Ramushwana reported for EWN that parties were in the final stages of their coalition negotiations and that Johannesburg’s new mayor is now expected to be elected on Friday. He reported that Ekurhuleni’s council will sit on Wednesday and Tshwane’s only on Thursday.
